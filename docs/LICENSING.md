and distribution in source or object form, including commercial use. It includes an express patent
license from contributors for applicable contribution claims and conditions that patent grant in

When redistributing the work or a derivative, review the actual license requirements. In
particular, distributors generally need to:

- provide a copy of Apache-2.0;
- mark modified files appropriately;
- retain applicable copyright, patent, trademark, and attribution notices; and
- preserve a readable copy of applicable notices from [`NOTICE`](../NOTICE).

Apache-2.0 does not require a private modification or hosted service to publish its source merely
because it is used over a network. Other obligations, contracts, dependency licenses, or laws can
still apply.

## Contributions

This repository does not currently declare a separate contributor license agreement. Intentionally
submitted contributions are handled under Apache-2.0's contribution terms unless the submission is
conspicuously marked otherwise. Follow [`CONTRIBUTING.md`](../CONTRIBUTING.md) and ensure you have
the right to submit the code, tests, documentation, assets, and third-party material involved.

Do not copy code or assets with incompatible or unknown terms. A future CLA or developer
certificate policy would need to be adopted explicitly and prospectively; it must not be implied by
this page.

## Commercial components and services

The Apache-2.0 community license does not require a future commercial extension, hosted service,
support agreement, or SLA to use the same commercial terms. Those separately distributed items may
have their own agreement, but they must not be included in or required by the community build
without a clear license and dependency boundary.

The community edition remains independently buildable and useful. See
[the open-core model](OPEN_CORE_MODEL.md) for the technical boundary. A commercial agreement does
not remove Apache-2.0 rights in the community code a recipient already receives under that license.

## Trademarks

Copyright/patent permission is not trademark permission. “Lab Platform” and project logos identify
the upstream project and official distributions. The separate [`TRADEMARKS.md`](../TRADEMARKS.md)
policy allows truthful compatibility/origin references but prohibits confusing claims of official
status, sponsorship, certification, or SLA.

Modified distributions should use a distinct name and clearly state that they are modified. Keep
required license/notice attribution. Permission to redistribute unmodified source does not by
itself authorize a confusing hosted-service name or use of project logos.

## Third-party components

Python, npm, container-base, operating-system, driver, firmware, and optional object-store
dependencies retain their own licenses and notices. Release SBOMs help inventory them but do not
perform a legal compatibility review. Distributors should:

1. inspect the locked dependency set and release SBOM;
2. include required third-party notices/source offers;
3. review optional plugins, firmware, fonts, and images separately; and
4. repeat that review when dependencies or container bases change.

Do not add a custom “source available” license to community files or describe it as Apache-2.0.
Questions about a particular distribution or commercial arrangement require qualified legal
advice.
